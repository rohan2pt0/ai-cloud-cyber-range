# Gap assessment — RBI (NBFCs – Cybersecurity, Technology: Risk, Resilience and Assurance Framework) Directions, 2026

> Paragraph-level assessment of the estate against the Reserve Bank of India NBFC Cybersecurity, Technology: Risk, Resilience and Assurance Framework Directions, 2026. 'entity' rows are obligations of the regulated NBFC that software cannot discharge.

Source: [RBI (NBFCs – Cybersecurity, Technology: Risk, Resilience and Assurance Framework) Directions, 2026](https://rbi.org.in) — RBI/DoS/2026-27/461, 31 July 2026 (in force with immediate effect); read 2026-10-14. Applies to NBFCs only (not to banks); the chapter that applies depends on the NBFC's layer and asset size, so the chapter is a column here. Housing finance companies report incidents to NHB, not RBI. The text was read from the copy supplied by the user.

Applicability scope: all layers / categories (use `--category` to narrow).

**Software-scope coverage: 47.1%** of 34 requirements software can address (✅ 1 supported · 🟡 30 partial · ⬜ 3 gap), plus **21 obligations that belong to the regulated entity** (🏛, never counted as covered by software) and 3 not applicable (➖).

> This is **not** a compliance certification and a tool cannot make an entity "100% compliant"; it shows what the estate demonstrably does, what it does not, and what only the entity can do.

## Backlog — what to build or fix, highest priority first

- 🟡 **128, 133-135** (high) Recovery meets RTO/RPO; near-zero RPO for critical systems; backups restored periodically; integrity preserved, access-controlled — Restore is proven (backup_restore, etcd restore in a game day; measured restore 12.5 s on the twin). Backups are a periodic tar.gz — RPO equals the backup interval, not near-zero — and archives are not encrypted or integrity-protected. **Next:** Encrypt backups (age/GPG) with a checksum manifest, add continuous/WAL archiving for Postgres, and state the RTO/RPO the ITSC approved.
- 🟡 **21(8), 109-110** (high) Audit trails exist for IT assets: detailed enough for audit, forensics, dispute resolution, non-repudiation; unauthorised activity recorded — Every gateway decision is one JSON audit line (verified end to end by the purple loop). With authentication on it names the verified subject, roles, auth method and source address, and refusals are audited with reason (the SOC raises `auth-bruteforce` on repeats). Still not tamper-evident (no hash chain / signing) and retention is whatever the log shipper keeps. **Next:** Hash-chain the audit lines and anchor the head in the evidence pack; enforce and prove retention ≥180 days (CERT-In) and the longer period the entity's policy sets.
- 🟡 **59(3), 101-103** (high) Backup sites for critical systems; physical/environmental controls; DC and DR geographically separate; e-surveillance — The lifecycle can stand up a cloud site (tree/canopy) as an alternate to the on-prem burrow, but no DR site is configured, and Mumbai-region on-prem plus a second site is not set up or tested. Physical and environmental controls are the entity's. **Next:** Define a DR pair (burrow in Mumbai + a cloud region in a different seismic zone), keep its config identical through GitOps, and rehearse it.
- 🟡 **59(4), 129-132** (high) Test BCP/DR at least annually (half-yearly for critical systems) with worst-case scenarios; full-working-day switch to DR; re-test after issues; Board sees results and gap analysis — Game days inject real failures into the burrow twin (node loss, control-plane restart, etcd restore) with SLOs and a report. They are not a switch-over to a DR site for a business day, are not scheduled, and are not reported to the Board. **Next:** Schedule half-yearly drills via `rhythm`, add a 'DR day' scenario that runs the workload from the second site, and produce the Board-ready gap analysis.
- 🟡 **7-9** (high) Base-layer (<₹500 cr) basics: access controls, roles, maker-checker, backup with periodic testing, BCP policy — Backup with restore test and a human-approval step for agent write actions exist; authentication/roles on the gateway do not (see 113-115). **Next:** Add caller authentication + roles to the gateway (see 113), then re-assess.
- ⬜ **21(9)** (medium) Public Key Infrastructure for confidentiality, access control, integrity, authentication, non-repudiation — No PKI use in the estate beyond TLS to providers; the evidence pack carries SHA-256 sums but is not digitally signed. **Next:** Sign evidence packs and release artefacts (cosign/minisign); support mTLS between gateway and router.
- ⬜ **92-93** (medium) Avoid unsupported hardware/software; monitor end-of-support and AMC dates; technology-refresh plan — Versions are pinned, but nothing tracks end-of-support dates of images, OS or runtimes. **Next:** Add an end-of-life check (endoflife.date snapshot kept offline) to `./securax scan` and the fitness ratchets.
- 🟡 **104-106** (medium) Vendor maintenance and support in contract; source code (or escrow); certificate that the application is free of known vulnerabilities, malware and covert channels, on each material change — SecuRAX is open source (source in hand). For its own releases CI runs SAST, secret scanning, Trivy, a CycloneDX SBOM; the 'free of malware/covert channels' written confirmation is a vendor attestation that does not exist. **Next:** Generate a release attestation (SBOM + scan results + signed provenance) per release; the entity still obtains it from its vendors.
- 🟡 **109** (medium) Every system that can access or affect sensitive information has audit logging — True for the AI gateway; not for the other organs (databases, router, workflow engine), whose logs depend on their own configuration. **Next:** Inventory each organ's log source and ship them all to one store; prove it with a journey.
- 🟡 **115-116** (medium) Multi-factor authentication for privileged users of critical systems and for remote/teleworking access; identify remote-access devices; secure data shown in teleworking — At the gateway, admin and auditor roles require an MFA-proven token (amr/acr from the identity provider); machine keys can never hold those roles. MFA itself is performed by the entity's identity provider. Not covered: other consoles and remote access (VPN/Tailscale SSO), identifying all remote-access devices, teleworking data controls. **Next:** Front the remaining consoles with the same IdP and require MFA; device inventory for remote access.
- 🟡 **117** (medium) Vendor risk assessment: concentration risk, conflicts, single point of failure, legal compliance, high availability, supply-chain risk — The `vet` gate assesses third-party code (pins, licence, executable surface, tamper detection) and installers are now hash-pinned; the provider ladder and local model avoid a single AI provider. No vendor questionnaire/risk register. **Next:** Vendor register with criticality, data categories, location and exit plan.
- 🟡 **118** (medium) Strong cryptography: algorithms, key lengths, protocols not deprecated — Cloud tree/data volume use KMS encryption and TLS to providers; local compose traffic is plain HTTP on loopback; backups are unencrypted; no crypto policy check. **Next:** Add a TLS/cipher policy check (testssl) to the sandbox and require TLS on any non-loopback listener.
- 🟡 **121-124** (medium) VA every six months and PT every 12 months of critical / DMZ systems, by independent experts, throughout the lifecycle, in production — The range, purple loop, garak/promptfoo and Trivy provide continuous internal testing of the AI estate. The regulator requires independent, trained testers and production testing: that is an engagement the entity commissions. **Next:** Produce a VAPT-ready scope pack (assets, endpoints, test accounts) from the genome for the external tester.
- 🟡 **137** (medium) Configuration and patch level identical at DC and DR — GitOps keeps one definition for every site (the twin cannot drift from the hardware definition); no automated DC-vs-DR drift report for a real pair. **Next:** Drift report comparing image digests/versions across sites in `./securax status`.
- 🟡 **21(1), 91** (medium) Inventory of information assets, classified by confidentiality/integrity/availability criticality — Estate components and AI models are inventoried (stages, organ registry, model registry); there is no enterprise asset/CMDB feed and no C/I/A classification of the entity's own assets. **Next:** Emit an asset inventory (components, images, models, data stores) with a C/I/A tag and owner from the genome; accept an import of the entity's CMDB.
- 🟡 **21(3), 113-114** (medium) Role-based access, need-based access, supervised and logged privileged access — The gateway now authenticates every caller (OIDC/JWT from any provider, or hashed service keys), maps them to roles (user, analyst, auditor, admin, service), enforces roles per endpoint, locks a source out after repeated failures, and names the verified subject in every audit line — proven end to end in the real container. Off by default on a laptop. Not covered: the other consoles (Open WebUI, n8n, Grafana...) have their own logins; no periodic access-review or privileged-session recording; the identity provider itself is the entity's. **Next:** Turn it on for the deployment (`gateway-auth enable` + OIDC settings); add a periodic access/token review report and privileged-session logging.
- 🟡 **21(7), 139-142** (medium) Incident management: define incidents; prevent, detect, analyse, respond; written procedures and roles; classification — AI-SOC classifies gateway events, the purple loop measures prevented/logged/alerted, `incident` runs the reporting clock and a tamper-evident case log. Severity is not mapped to the regulator's incident categories and procedures are not approved by the entity. **Next:** Map SOC severities to the RBI/CERT-In incident categories and emit them on each case.
- 🟡 **23, 125-126** (medium) Vulnerability management process; time-bound fixing; documented VA/PT approach with CVSS scoring, including cloud — Trivy/SAST/secret scans in CI, OSV and the CISA KEV catalogue offline, KEV report in `scan`. No fixed remediation SLA per severity and no CVSS-based scoring record. **Next:** Track findings with CVSS + KEV flag and age; fail the build when a KEV item is older than the entity's SLA.
- 🟡 **24, 147-148** (medium) Cybersecurity preparedness indicators; metrics for performance, recovery, RTO/RPO; IT performance and maturity scorecard — Fitness functions, game-day SLOs and coverage maps give measurable indicators. They are for this estate, not an enterprise scorecard, and there is no independent audit of them. **Next:** Publish a quarterly indicator pack (latency/ReDoS budget, MTTD/MTTR from the purple loop, restore time) from `./securax evidence`.
- 🟡 **25-27** (medium) Cyber Crisis Management Plan; protect against zero-day, remote access, ransomware, DDoS, phishing, BEC, vishing, identity fraud — The purple loop exercises AI-specific attacks (prompt injection, exfiltration, tool coercion, PII harvesting). DDoS, ransomware, phishing and BEC are outside this estate; the CCMP itself is the entity's. **Next:** Add ransomware (backup-targeting) and DDoS (rate-limit) scenarios to the game days.
- 🟡 **28, 141** (medium) Report cyber incidents to RBI on the DAKSH platform within six hours of detection; notify CERT-In proactively — `./securax incident` starts the 6-hour clock at detection for every applicable regulator at once (RBI/DAKSH, CERT-In, SEBI... regulators are data), flags overdue (exit code for cron/n8n), drafts each notification with the case facts and the gaps, records filings as on-time/late, keeps a hash-chained case log and seals evidence. It does not file anything (DAKSH has no integration here) and nothing pages a human by itself. **Next:** Wire `incident due` to a pager/e-mail/n8n alert; optional DAKSH field export once the portal's schema is known.
- 🟡 **43-46** (medium) IT-enabled MIS; regulatory returns system-driven; read-only access for RBI supervisors — Security metrics and incident summaries feed `./securax evidence`; business MIS, returns and supervisor access are the entity's core systems. There is no read-only auditor/supervisor role (see 113). **Next:** Add a read-only auditor role to the evidence/audit endpoints once authentication exists.
- 🟡 **47-58, 149-154** (medium) IS Audit: framework approved by Audit Committee, skilled personnel, independence, annual, findings to Board/ACB, audit-mode access, CAATs / continuous auditing — Audit framework, independence and findings handling are the entity's. SecuRAX supports CAATs/continuous auditing with scripted, repeatable checks (verify, fitness, diffcheck, compliance maps) and an evidence pack. No audit-mode (read-only) access role exists. **Next:** Auditor read-only role + a 'show me the control, the test and the last result' view per requirement.
- ⬜ **136** (low) Documented reconciliation methodology when RPO is non-zero — Not documented. **Next:** Write the reconciliation procedure for chat/workflow data after a restore, and test it in the restore journey.
- 🟡 **107-108** (low) Capacity management: systems support business functions; annual capacity assessment consistent with peaks and projections — Elastic-resource handling and capacity/footprint budgets are tested (laptop and cluster); no peak-trend capacity plan. **Next:** Record peak and trend metrics in the evidence pack.
