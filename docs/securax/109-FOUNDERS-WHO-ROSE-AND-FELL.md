**The ask:** learn from successful founders (Shantanu Deshpande, Ashneer Grover, Nikhil Kamath, Ronnie Screwvala and others) and
from founders who failed, in India and on every continent, so SecuRAX does not repeat their mistakes.

**What was built:**
- Three more monthly guards in `./securax venture guards`: debt, undisclosed related-party payments, and a current key-person
  handover note.
- With doc 108, the guards now check twelve things a month.

**How people are described:** they are real, so their legal status is stated exactly, as one of:
- **convicted** (a court found guilt);
- **regulator order** or **court finding** (not a criminal conviction);
- **charged** (not proven);
- **alleged** (claimed, not proven, often denied).

A settlement is not a finding of guilt. Facts are from public reporting read on 2026-10-22 (section 5).

## 1. Indian founders: what to copy, what to avoid

| Founder | What happened | Copy | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nithin and Nikhil Kamath** (Zerodha, 2010) | No outside investors; growth by word of mouth, not ads. After SEBI's 2024 derivatives rules, FY25 revenue fell to ₹8,847 Cr from ₹9,993 Cr. Nithin had a stroke in 2024 and then spoke openly about succession | Profit first; trust as the growth engine | One rule set carrying the revenue: model the "regulator changes the rules" case every quarter, and plan for a founder's health |
| **Sridhar Vembu** (Zoho) | Never raised venture capital; rural offices; stepped down as CEO in January 2025 to lead research | Not raising means not being forced to sell; hand over roles while you can still choose to | A burst of patriotic sign-ups (Arattai, 2025) is not retention |
| **Shantanu Deshpande** (Bombay Shaving Co.) | Loss ₹80 Cr (FY23) to ₹62 Cr (FY24) to ₹9 Cr (FY26, by the company's own "adjusted" measure); unusually frank in public | Candour about the numbers; cutting burn steadily | Treating "adjusted" profit as cash |
| **Ronnie Screwvala** | Sold UTV to Disney (2012); then upGrad and the Swades Foundation | Reinvention planned, not stumbled into; an exit can pay for the next build and for giving back | — |
| **Kunal Shah** | Sold Freecharge (2015); founded CRED (2018) for a customer group he understood well | Sell at a high point; build for people you know | — |
| **Deepinder Goyal** (Zomato) | Cut about 10% of staff and closed a market in 2015 to lower burn; admitted mistakes in writing; stepped down as group CEO in 2026 | Cut early and openly; write down the mistakes | — |
| **Falguni Nayar** (Nykaa) | Founded at about 50 (2012); listed in 2021 | Starting late is fine; varied, patient investors | — |
| **Ashneer Grover** (BharatPe) | Resigned in 2022 amid a dispute with the board; the company **alleged** misuse of money; a police FIR was registered (2023); the parties **settled** in September 2024. No court found him guilty | Speed to market | Public conduct and family on the payroll became the story: a risk any board will write down |
| **Bhavish Aggarwal** (Ola Electric) | A consumer-regulator notice over 10,000+ complaints (2024); a public spat with a critic; sales fell sharply in 2025 | — | Selling past service capacity; arguing with customers in public |
| **Byju Raveendran** (Byju's) | The auditor and investor directors left (2023); a US default judgment he disputes; a Singapore **contempt** sentence (May 2026, not fraud), which he says was procedural | — | Losing every independent check; ignoring court orders |

## 2. Founders who fell, on every continent

| Where | Person | Status | The cause, in one line | The lesson |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| India | Ramalinga Raju (Satyam) | **Convicted** (2015) | Profits and cash that did not exist | Let others confirm your cash with the bank directly |
| India | Vijay Mallya (Kingfisher) | **Declared a fugitive economic offender** (2019) | Borrowed to grow while losing money | No debt to run the business |
| India | Rana Kapoor (Yes Bank) | **Charged**, on trial | **Alleged** money to family firms tied to a bank deal | Keep family companies far from any deal |
| India | Subrata Roy (Sahara) | **Court and regulator orders**; died 2023 | Raised public money outside the rules | Never collect money in a way the regulator has not approved |
| India | Nirav Modi (PNB) | **Charged**; extradition ordered | **Alleged** fraud on bank guarantees | Paper is not trust |
| India | Chanda Kochhar (ICICI) | **Tribunal finding** (prima facie, 2025); not convicted | Undeclared conflict of interest | Declare every family interest in writing, before the decision |
| North America | Elizabeth Holmes (Theranos) | **Convicted** (2022) | Claimed technology that did not work | Never show what does not work |
| North America | Sam Bankman-Fried (FTX) | **Convicted** (2023–24) | Customer money in his own firm | Customer funds and data stay separate, with logs |
| North America | Adam Neumann (WeWork) | No charges | Deals between himself and the company | Avoid and disclose self-dealing |
| North America | Trevor Milton (Nikola) | **Convicted**, later pardoned; company bankrupt (2025) | Overstated the product | A pardon does not save a company |
| North America | Jeffrey Skilling (Enron) | **Convicted** (2006) | Accounts that hid debt | If you cannot explain it simply, do not book it |
| North America | Charlie Javice (Frank) | **Convicted** (2025, appealing) | Faked user numbers for a sale | Due diligence will find it |
| Europe | Markus Braun (Wirecard) | **Charged**; trial ongoing; denies | Cash said to be held by third parties | Check third-party cash yourself |
| Europe / Asia | Carlos Ghosn (Nissan) | **Charged** in Japan; fled (2019); says he is innocent | Pay set by one person alone | No one sets their own pay |
| Asia | Do Kwon (Terraform) | **Convicted** (2025) | A "stable" claim that failed under stress | A claim must survive stress |
| Asia | Jack Ma (Ant Group) | **Regulator action**, not fraud: IPO halted (2020), fine (2023) | Mocked regulators in public | Talk with regulators, do not taunt them |
| Asia | Hui Ka Yan (Evergrande) | **Regulator order** (2024): lifetime market ban | Debt plus inflated revenue | Debt and dressed-up numbers end the same way |
| Asia | Kim Woo-choong (Daewoo) | **Convicted** (2006), pardoned | An empire built on debt | Grow from earnings |
| Africa | Markus Jooste (Steinhoff) | **Regulator order** (2024); never charged; died 2024 | Misstated reports, 2014–2017 | Independent checks on the reports |
| Africa | Dash (Ghana) | **Alleged** inflated users and a cash shortfall; shut 2023 | Numbers not checked | Every reported number traces to a record |
| Australia | Alan Bond | **Convicted** (1990s) | Took money from a company he controlled | Company money is not the founder's |

## 3. What SecuRAX does about it

**Checked every month by `./securax venture guards`:**

| Guard | Line | From |
|---|---|---|
| Cash runway | 12 months or more | Dunzo, Byju's (doc 108) |
| Burn multiple | 2 or less | WeWork (doc 108) |
| Largest product / customer share | 50% / 30% | Nokia, Zerodha's rule-set risk |
| Revenue with a payment reference | every line | Raju, Javice, Builder.ai |
| Independent review of the books | yearly, from ₹20 lakh of revenue | Byju's, Steinhoff |
| Claims the code cannot prove | none | Holmes, Milton, Builder.ai |
| Stop tests past their date | none | doc 107 |
| Platform shifts reviewed | every 92 days | Nokia, Zerodha |
| **Debt to run the business** | none | Mallya, Evergrande, Daewoo |
| **Related-party payments without disclosure** | none | Kapoor (charged), Kochhar (finding), Neumann, BluSmart |
| **A current handover note** (keys, accounts, customers, who to call) | reviewed yearly | Kamath's stroke, Ghosn |

**Habits the owner keeps (the guards cannot check them):**

1. **A 24-hour rule** before replying to any public criticism; never argue with a customer in public (Aggarwal, Grover).
2. **Answer every notice inside its deadline**, from any regulator, court or tax office; keep a compliance calendar for GST,
   company filings and DPDP (Sahara, Byju's, Ant).
3. **Two outside advisers and a quarterly review** before any outside money; treat an auditor resigning as an emergency (Byju's).
4. **A decision log** (date, options, why) for every price, hire and legal choice (Goyal's written mistakes).
5. **A yearly memo:** what to stop, sell or start (Screwvala, Shah).
6. **A short monthly note** of revenue, burn and lessons, even if only you read it (Deshpande).
7. **Customer data stays on the customer's machines** (SBF); SecuRAX is self-hosted by design.
8. **Honour every existing duty first:** no one else's time, devices or information goes into SecuRAX, and every permission
   is in writing before any commercial step.

## 4. Feeding the new guards

- `./securax venture plan debt=0`
- `./securax venture log money 25000 "office rent" --related "family-owned premises" --disclosed "adviser e-mail 2027-01-05"`
- Write `vault/handover.md` (private): keys, accounts, customers, who to call. Review it every year.
