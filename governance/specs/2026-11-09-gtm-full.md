# Ideas from a public wearable launch (no-screen, "hears only the wearer" voice companion), 2026-11-09. Cues only, in our words; no hardware.
- lifeos-tap-capture (2026-12-31, open models + Claude review): capture a thought, symptom, meal or reminder in one gesture with no typing: push-to-talk from the Life OS home-screen shortcut or a notification action, a short haptic buzz when listening starts and when it is saved (haptics-calm), speech-to-text on the device or the owner's local model, sorted into Inbox, Health log, Reminders or People notes. - lifeos-bystander-safe (2026-12-31, strong tier): the 'hears only the owner' principle in software: capture runs only while the owner holds or taps; no ambient listening, no recording of meetings or people nearby; a visible and haptic cue whenever the microphone is live; a test that no code path opens the microphone without a user gesture. # The "one live body number" idea (a wearable's trademarked cost-of-being-alive index), 2026-11-09. Cues only; never use their trademarked name; their accuracy claims are the company's own, not peer-reviewed.
- principle floor-and-ceiling (add to governance/principles.json, core false): for any living system, track two numbers, not one: the floor (what it costs at rest: lower it every day) and the ceiling (what it can reach under load: raise it over time), and say in one line what moved them today. Applies to the body (resting cost, peak capacity), the estate (idle resource use, peak throughput) and money (fixed monthly cost, earning capacity).
- lifeos-one-number (2027-02-28, open models + Claude review): one honest daily body score in the Life OS with its day's minimum and maximum and the times they happened, a plain sentence on what moved it ("breathwork this morning brought your rest number to its lowest this month"), and a 30-day trend. Built from data the owner already has (resting heart rate, HRV, sleep, steps, logged sessions) using open, explainable formulas; every score shows how it is computed; not medical advice.
- adopt-open-wearables (2027-01-31, scout candidate, giants principle): evaluate Open Wearables (MIT, self-hosted; aggregates wearable data and computes open sleep, recovery and resilience scores) as the backend behind wearable-import and lifeos-one-number; run vet; adopt behind our own adapter with an exit, or keep our own formulas if it fails a gate.
- gadgetbridge-import (2027-02-28): Gadgetbridge (AGPL-3.0, Android) talks to many watches without a vendor cloud; keep it a separate app (no code linked into SecuRAX, licence) and import its exported database or CSV through wearable-import.
- open-hardware-note (2027-03-31): document Bangle.js 2 (hackable JavaScript watch; one study found its steps and heart rate valid in daily life) and PineTime with InfiniTime (cheapest, basic, no sleep tracking) as the open, vendor-free options for the owner's first wearable; no purchase without the owner's yes.

# Premium look and feel, so the product reads as private-bank grade (owner, 2026-11-09). HIGH PRIORITY: order these before the gtm content items.
- design-premium-language (2026-11-25, Claude designs, open models apply): one premium design language in governance/design.json (the single source; never per page), applied in slices to the Life OS, the product site, the Command Center and the kit apps:
  * restraint: generous whitespace, fewer elements per screen, one action per view (calm screens, doc 100);
  * typography: a self-hosted display serif for headings and a refined sans for text (offline, no font CDN), a strict type scale, tabular figures for numbers;
  * colour: a deep 'obsidian and ivory' pair with one precious accent (the brand gold used sparingly, like leaf, not paint), muted status colours, every pair still WCAG AA;
  * material: fine hairline borders, soft layered shadows, subtle glass on overlays, no flat grey boxes;
  * motion: short spring-eased transitions (150-250 ms) on state changes only, a calm number count-up on scores, all off under prefers-reduced-motion and battery saver;
  * touch: haptics on the moments that matter (haptics-calm), 44 px targets, press states that feel physical;
  * voice and copy: a concierge tone (short, warm, certain), no jargon, no exclamation marks;
  * graphics: one consistent icon set and a few bespoke illustrations or dial visuals (like the one-number gauge) instead of stock imagery.
- design-premium-showcase (2026-11-28): redo three hero screens first, where buyers judge quality: the product site home, the Life OS Today screen, and the Command Center door; before/after screenshots at phone and laptop width in both themes go in the PR for the owner's yes.
- design-premium-review (2026-11-28): a premium review checklist in doc 100 (restraint, type, colour, material, motion, touch, copy, graphics, speed under one second), run by Claude on screenshots before any UI merge; the real-browser audit still gates contrast, errors and overflow.
- design-premium-onboarding (2026-12-20): white-glove first run: a short, beautiful welcome, the owner's name and goals in three taps, everything works as a guest, and a 'your estate is ready' moment; privacy shown as a luxury (what never leaves your device).
