- gtm-partnerships (2027-01-31): audit firms (white-label), banking institutes (course licensing), security vendors (sponsorship), associations (bulk training); each checked for conflicts with the owner's employer first; run by the commercial lead.

# Ideas from a public wearable launch (no-screen, "hears only the wearer" voice companion), 2026-11-09. Cues only, in our words; no hardware.
- lifeos-tap-capture (2026-12-31, open models + Claude review): capture a thought, symptom, meal or reminder in one gesture with no typing: push-to-talk from the Life OS home-screen shortcut or a notification action, a short haptic buzz when listening starts and when it is saved (haptics-calm), speech-to-text on the device or the owner's local model, sorted into Inbox, Health log, Reminders or People notes. Never an always-on microphone.
- lifeos-bystander-safe (2026-12-31, strong tier): the 'hears only the owner' principle in software: capture runs only while the owner holds or taps; no ambient listening, no recording of meetings or people nearby; a visible and haptic cue whenever the microphone is live; a test that no code path opens the microphone without a user gesture. Add it to governance/principles.json as part of privacy.
- lifeos-health-log (2027-01-31): a private health log (headache, dizziness, sleep, meals, workouts, medication taken) with pattern spotting by the local model only, never a cloud model; plain summaries to share with a doctor on request; medication reminders. Not medical advice, said on the screen.
- lifeos-people-memory (2027-01-31): remember what matters about people (birthdays, anniversaries, 'she mentioned wanting to visit Tokyo') as editable Memories (settings-account-control), surfaced at the right time; private, exportable, deletable.
- wearable-import (2027-03-31, giants principle): read health signals from the phone's own health store exports (Health Connect, Apple Health, open formats) and later any wearable with an open API, behind one adapter with an exit; no vendor lock-in, nothing leaves the device without consent.
- e2e-sync-check (2026-12-15, strong tier): confirm the Life OS sync is end-to-end encrypted so even the server cannot read it ('we cannot sell what we cannot see'); if not, threat-model it and queue the fix; say it plainly on the privacy screen.

# The "one live body number" idea (a wearable's trademarked cost-of-being-alive index), 2026-11-09. Cues only; never use their trademarked name; their accuracy claims are the company's own, not peer-reviewed.
- principle floor-and-ceiling (add to governance/principles.json, core false): for any living system, track two numbers, not one: the floor (what it costs at rest: lower it every day) and the ceiling (what it can reach under load: raise it over time), and say in one line what moved them today. Applies to the body (resting cost, peak capacity), the estate (idle resource use, peak throughput) and money (fixed monthly cost, earning capacity).
- lifeos-one-number (2027-02-28, open models + Claude review): one honest daily body score in the Life OS with its day's minimum and maximum and the times they happened, a plain sentence on what moved it ("breathwork this morning brought your rest number to its lowest this month"), and a 30-day trend. Built from data the owner already has (resting heart rate, HRV, sleep, steps, logged sessions) using open, explainable formulas; every score shows how it is computed; not medical advice.
- adopt-open-wearables (2027-01-31, scout candidate, giants principle): evaluate Open Wearables (MIT, self-hosted; aggregates wearable data and computes open sleep, recovery and resilience scores) as the backend behind wearable-import and lifeos-one-number; run vet; adopt behind our own adapter with an exit, or keep our own formulas if it fails a gate.
- gadgetbridge-import (2027-02-28): Gadgetbridge (AGPL-3.0, Android) talks to many watches without a vendor cloud; keep it a separate app (no code linked into SecuRAX, licence) and import its exported database or CSV through wearable-import.
- open-hardware-note (2027-03-31): document Bangle.js 2 (hackable JavaScript watch; one study found its steps and heart rate valid in daily life) and PineTime with InfiniTime (cheapest, basic, no sleep tracking) as the open, vendor-free options for the owner's first wearable; no purchase without the owner's yes.
