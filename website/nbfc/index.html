The problem

In force on day one, and it keeps recurring.

The Directions repealed the 2023 IT governance direction and applied at once. Most of the work is not a one-off project.

Which paragraphs are yours?

What applies depends on your layer and asset size: Chapter III, IV or V. A consultant's spreadsheet often mixes them; readiness shows only your chapter.

Duties on a calendar

Vulnerability assessment every six months, penetration testing every year, an annual IS audit and IT risk assessment, and for the middle layer a quarterly CISO review with the Board.

Six hours

A cyber incident goes to RBI on the DAKSH platform within six hours of detection, with CERT-In notified. The clock starts long before anyone writes the report.

How it works

Four steps, in the order a Board asks.

1. Pick your chapter

Start an assessment for Chapter III, IV or V. Every applicable requirement starts as "not assessed", never as met.

2. Mark with evidence

Met, partly met, gap or not applicable. A "met" needs the evidence that proves it; every mark keeps who made it and when.

3. Act on the vital few

The five decisions that matter most come first: critical gaps such as privileged MFA, the CISO's independence and the IS audit, and anything past its date.

4. Hand over the board pack (print it to PDF) and the auditor's sheet (CSV), and record each VA, PT, audit and Board review as it happens, so the next one never slips.

What it does today, and what is next

Built and tested, or not claimed.

Built

Requirements per chapter, paraphrased from the Directions with their paragraph numbers; marks with evidence and provenance; the vital few; recurring-duty dates; the board pack and auditor's sheet; the six-hour incident clock for RBI and CERT-In (./securax incident).

Next

A browser screen for marking (today it is a command line), attaching evidence files, sign-off by a second person, and importing the workbook your consultant already uses.

Not claimed

It is a self-assessment tool, not an audit opinion and not a certification. It does not make you compliant; your Board, policies and controls do. It shows, with evidence, how far you are.

Questions NBFC teams ask

Does any of our data leave our machine?

No. The assessment, the evidence notes and the board pack stay on the machine you run it on, readable only by you. It makes no network call.

Is the text of the Directions in the tool?

No. Each requirement is paraphrased with its paragraph numbers, so you can check it against the Directions themselves. Read the Directions for the exact wording.

We are a housing finance company. Does it apply?

The requirements largely do, but housing finance companies report incidents to NHB, not RBI. Check the reporting route for your registration.

Is this an audit?

No. It is a self-assessment that your IS auditor can read and test. The report says so on every page.

We are not an NBFC. Can we use it?

Yes. The same engine runs on every regulation SecuRAX maps: SEBI CSCRF by category (--map cscrf --category small), the UK NCSC CAF, NIST AI 600-1, the MIT AI risk domains and post-quantum readiness. NBFCs come first because their Directions took effect with no transition period.
